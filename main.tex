\begin{abstract}
Morphology-based screening of unseen treatments must decide not only which response group a sample belongs to, but whether the prediction can be trusted and how much imaging is worth acquiring. We formulate this as a reliability-aware policy that accepts a prediction, aggregates additional replicate wells, or defers it for re-measurement, and evaluate it for 37 immune and vascular stimuli in public RxRx2 HUVEC Cell Painting data with entire treatments held out. A 6~KB Logistic model on released embeddings reaches balanced accuracy 0.442, but chemokine and angiogenic responses are nearly indistinguishable. Under a treatment-level paired bootstrap, aggregating six replicate wells improves balanced accuracy by 0.049 (95\% CI 0.005--0.096), and nested confidence-based deferral lowers accepted-case error by 0.046 (0.005--0.089) at 71\% coverage. Experiment shift compounds treatment shift, reducing balanced accuracy to 0.374 even for treatments seen in training. Reducing raw-image input from six to three channels lowers balanced accuracy from 0.398 to 0.362 while leaving inference compute almost unchanged, so its benefit lies in staining, acquisition, and storage cost.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Cell Painting, high-content screening, treatment shift, selective prediction, batch effects, lightweight inference.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
Cell Painting converts cellular responses to perturbations into multiplexed images that support mechanism-oriented screening, but its signal can be confounded by experimental and imaging variation \cite{cimini2023,arevalo2024}. In the immune phenomics work associated with RxRx2, soluble factors with related function showed related morphology \cite{cuccarese2020}. A practical screening pipeline must therefore decide not only which response group is predicted, but whether the prediction is reliable enough to act on. The question sharpens as assays move toward compact, lower-cost imagers: each fluorescence channel adds a stain, light source, filter set, and exposure time, so channel count drives device cost, acquisition time, and data volume.

Prior workflows emphasize replicate reproducibility and batch correction \cite{bray2016,cimini2023,arevalo2024}. Screening a previously unseen soluble factor raises a distinct operational question: when should a low-cost prediction be accepted, averaged across additional wells, or deferred for re-measurement? Our contribution is therefore a screening \emph{policy} rather than a new classifier (Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}), with every decision evaluated on stimuli never seen during training. We contribute: 1) an expert-reviewed four-group mapping of 37 stimuli (Table~\ref{tab:mapping}) with group-specific reporting that exposes biases hidden by aggregate scores; 2) a treatment-held-out evaluation of replicate-well aggregation, plate awareness, and nested confidence-based deferral with paired confidence intervals, under both treatment and experiment shift; and 3) a raw-image 6/3/2-channel ablation that separates acquisition burden from inference cost. The individual controls are established techniques; our aim is to quantify how far each can be trusted when combined for unseen stimuli.

\section{Materials and Methods}
\subsection{Data and functional labels}
We use released RxRx2 HUVEC metadata and image embeddings \cite{rxrx2}, and the raw six-channel images of all sites of the analysed treatments. Functional labels were drafted by a pharmacy student and confirmed by a professor of pharmacy (Table~\ref{tab:mapping}); type-III interferons (IL-28A, IL-28B, IL-29) were excluded because of limited expected HUVEC responsiveness. Each treatment is applied at six concentrations in two experiments (HUVEC-1, HUVEC-2), giving 444 conditions with six replicate wells each and 10,642 image sites on 24 plates.

\subsection{Evaluation protocols and models}
All experiments hold out entire treatments. \emph{Embedding experiments} use five-fold stratified group cross-validation with treatment as the group. \emph{Raw-image experiments} use one fixed split of 23 training, 5 validation, and 9 test treatments, covering every group, because repeated CNN training was computationally impractical; absolute scores from the two protocols are not directly comparable. We report macro-F1, balanced accuracy (BA), and expected calibration error (ECE). Confidence intervals use a treatment-level cluster bootstrap (1,000 resamples); comparisons evaluate both settings on the same resampled treatments, giving paired intervals.

On embeddings we benchmark standardized, class-weighted Logistic Regression; XGBoost \cite{chen2016} without class weighting (250 trees, depth 4, learning rate 0.06, row and column subsampling 0.85); and a small MLP (64 and 32 hidden units, class-balanced resampling, early stopping). Principal component analysis for dimensional reduction is fitted inside each training fold. Latency is measured per site on a laptop CPU (Intel Core Ultra 7 255H) together with the serialized model size. For raw images, we fine-tune ImageNet-pretrained MobileNetV3-Small \cite{howard2019} whose first convolution is replaced by a $C$-channel layer initialized from the channel-averaged RGB filter scaled by $3/C$, with a four-way classifier. Sites are resized to $224\times224$, augmented with random flips and $90^\circ$ rotations, and sampled with class-balanced weights. Training uses AdamW (learning rate and weight decay $10^{-4}$), batch size 32, and up to 25 epochs with early stopping (patience 5) on validation performance. Six channels are compared with DNA/RNA/mitochondria and DNA/mitochondria subsets, fixed before inspecting test results; six- and three-channel models use two seeds, and the two-channel model is exploratory with one.

\subsection{Reliability analyses}
\emph{Replicate aggregation} (Logistic): sites are averaged into wells and $k$ wells into a condition embedding; for $k\in\{1,2,3\}$ wells are drawn at random ten times, while $k=6$ uses all wells. Low and high strata are the two lowest and two highest of each treatment's six concentrations. \emph{Plate awareness} (Logistic): we compare same- versus cross-plate similarity of same-condition wells and evaluate unlabeled plate centering, which subtracts each plate's mean embedding. \emph{Deferral} (XGBoost, the best-calibrated model): a site is accepted when its maximum class probability exceeds $\tau$ \cite{geifman2017}. In each outer fold, one training treatment per group is withheld as a calibration set, and the lowest $\tau$ with calibration-set error at most 0.40 is applied to the unseen test fold; we also report the risk--coverage curve and its area (AURC). \emph{Diagnostics}: a three-class variant merging chemokine and angiogenic groups, a binary chemokine-versus-angiogenic classifier, and HUVEC-1$\rightarrow$HUVEC-2 transfer.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Embedding baselines and group-specific behavior}
Logistic Regression attains the highest BA (0.442) with a 6.1~KB model and 0.47~ms per site, while XGBoost attains the highest macro-F1 and the best calibration (Table~\ref{tab:embedding}). The small MLP is poorly calibrated (ECE 0.477) and therefore unsuitable for confidence-based deferral. Treatment-level intervals are wide and overlap across models, so the ranking should not be over-interpreted. Reducing the Logistic input to 32 principal components retains BA (0.446), whereas 16 components lower it to 0.431; the post-embedding decision layer can therefore be compact, although this does not reduce the cost of producing the embedding.

\subsection{Replicate aggregation under low concentration}
Averaging all six wells instead of one raises condition-level BA from 0.416 to 0.465 (Fig.~\ref{fig:replicates}), a paired gain of $+0.049$ (CI 0.005--0.096). The gain doubles in the low-concentration stratum, from 0.294 to 0.396 ($+0.103$, CI 0.017--0.190), but is small in the high stratum (0.499 to 0.509), so replicate acquisition is most useful where morphology signals are weak. The same concentration dependence appears at the single-site level, where XGBoost BA rises from 0.282 (low) to 0.477 (mid) and 0.550 (high).

\subsection{Plate awareness and confidence-based deferral}
Same-condition wells are more similar on the same plate (cosine 0.885) than across plates (0.862). Plate centering raises Logistic BA from 0.443 to 0.450 in an independent re-run, a small but consistent paired gain ($+0.007$, CI 0.002--0.012).

Pooling the five outer folds, nested deferral gives accepted-case error 0.404 at 70.9\% coverage versus 0.451 without deferral, a paired reduction of 0.046 (CI 0.005--0.089). The selection rule was unstable, however: with only four calibration treatments, selected thresholds ranged from 0.45 to 0.60 and coverage from 37\% to 81\% across folds, and accepted error exceeded the 0.40 target in two folds (0.438, 0.478). The pooled risk--coverage curve decreases steadily (Fig.~\ref{fig:riskcoverage}; AURC 0.302 versus overall error 0.452). Error approaches zero below about 15\% coverage, but about half of these sites come from four strong-response cytokines (IL-1$\alpha$, IL-1$\beta$, TNF-$\alpha$, IFN-$\omega$), so this regime reflects easy treatments rather than a safe operating point. We therefore treat deferral as exploratory re-measurement triage.

\subsection{Experiment shift compounds treatment shift}
Training on HUVEC-1 and testing on HUVEC-2 within the treatment-held-out folds lowers BA to 0.365 (Logistic), 0.344 (XGBoost), and 0.325 (MLP), versus 0.417--0.442 with both experiments pooled. Even when the same treatments appear in both experiments, BA reaches only 0.374 (Logistic) and 0.368 (XGBoost). Seen stimuli in a new experiment are thus recognized less reliably than unseen stimuli within pooled experiments, so practical screening faces both shifts at once.

\subsection{Reduced-channel raw-image screening}
The six-channel configuration was strongest and stable across seeds (Table~\ref{tab:channels}, Fig.~\ref{fig:channels}); reducing to DNA/RNA/mitochondria lowered BA from 0.398 to 0.362, and the single-seed two-channel result does not rank two versus three channels. Because only the first convolution depends on the channel count, all configurations have 1.52~M parameters, compute falls only from 60.9 to 55.5 and 53.7~MMACs, and single-thread CPU latency from 7.9 to 7.6 and 7.5~ms per site. The saving of reduced-channel imaging lies in stains, optics, acquisition time, and image data volume (one half or one third of the six-channel data), not in inference, and it costs measurable accuracy. Complementary multi-organelle information therefore remains beneficial for screening unseen treatments.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
Chemokine and angiogenic stimuli are confused regardless of classifier, so a screening policy should report group-specific reliability, and a three-group formulation may suit this cell type better. The reliability gains are modest and their intervals wide because only 37 treatments are available; they should be confirmed on larger panels, and the deferral threshold requires larger calibration sets before it can be fixed in advance. Only two HUVEC experiments are available, so experiment shift on independently acquired systems remains to be measured. The raw-image comparison uses a single split with nine test treatments; seed variation is small, but variation across alternative splits is not captured. All images come from a high-content research platform, so the channel ablation emulates, but does not demonstrate, a lower-cost imager. The system should be framed as candidate screening and re-measurement prioritization, not clinical diagnosis or definitive mechanism annotation.

\section{Conclusion}
For morphology-based screening of unseen stimuli, the central question is not only accuracy but when a prediction can be trusted. Under combined treatment and experiment shift, replicate aggregation and confidence-based deferral yield modest but statistically supported gains, plate centering adds a small consistent improvement, and group-specific reporting reveals that chemokine and angiogenic responses are not separable in HUVEC morphology. Reducing six channels to three or two lowers stain, optics, acquisition, and storage burden at a measurable accuracy cost while leaving inference cost unchanged. Future work should validate the policy prospectively on larger panels and lower-cost imaging systems.
