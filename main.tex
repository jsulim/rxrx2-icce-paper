\begin{abstract}
Morphology-based screening of unseen treatments must decide not only which response group a sample belongs to, but whether the prediction can be trusted and how much imaging is worth acquiring. We formulate this as a reliability-aware policy that accepts a prediction, aggregates additional replicate wells, or defers it for re-measurement, and evaluate it for 37 immune and vascular stimuli in public RxRx2 HUVEC Cell Painting data with entire treatments held out. A 6~KB Logistic model on released embeddings reaches balanced accuracy 0.442, but chemokine and angiogenic responses are nearly indistinguishable. Under treatment shift, a treatment-level paired bootstrap shows that aggregating six replicate wells improves balanced accuracy by 0.049 (95\% CI 0.005--0.096), and nested confidence-based deferral raises balanced accuracy of accepted predictions by 0.100 (0.048--0.120) at 53\% coverage. Combining three-well aggregation with deferral of the least confident quarter of conditions reaches accuracy comparable to imaging all six wells at half the wells, whereas sequential well-by-well early stopping performs worse. Under experiment shift, where models trained on one HUVEC experiment are tested on the other, both controls lose most of their benefit. Centering each experiment on its untreated control wells, which needs no labels, reduces the area under the balanced-error--coverage curve (by 0.059, 0.026--0.085) and returns the source-calibrated deferral threshold to a selective operating point, but yields no detectable aggregation gain. Reducing raw-image input from six to three channels lowers balanced accuracy from 0.398 to 0.362 while leaving inference compute almost unchanged, so its benefit lies in staining, acquisition, and storage cost.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Cell Painting converts cellular responses to perturbations into multiplexed images that support mechanism-oriented screening, but its signal can be confounded by experimental and imaging variation \cite{cimini2023,arevalo2024}. In the immune phenomics work associated with RxRx2, soluble factors with related function showed related morphology \cite{cuccarese2020}. A practical screening pipeline must therefore decide not only which response group is predicted, but whether the prediction is reliable enough to act on. The question sharpens as assays move toward compact, lower-cost imagers: each fluorescence channel adds a stain, light source, filter set, and exposure time, so channel count drives device cost, acquisition time, and data volume. Moreover, every new screening run is a new experimental batch, so a model trained on earlier runs always faces experiment shift in addition to novel treatments.

Prior workflows emphasize replicate reproducibility, batch correction, and normalization to control wells \cite{bray2016,caicedo2017,cimini2023,arevalo2024}. Screening a previously unseen soluble factor raises a distinct operational question: when should a low-cost prediction be accepted, averaged across additional wells, or deferred for re-measurement? Our contribution is therefore a screening \emph{policy} rather than a new classifier (Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}), evaluated on stimuli never seen during training. We contribute: 1) an expert-reviewed four-group mapping of 37 stimuli (Table~\ref{tab:mapping}) with group-specific reporting that exposes biases hidden by aggregate scores; 2) a treatment-held-out evaluation of replicate-well aggregation, plate and control-well centering, and nested confidence-based deferral, individually and as a combined aggregate-then-defer policy, with paired confidence intervals, reported separately under treatment shift and under experiment shift; and 3) a raw-image 6/3/2-channel ablation that separates acquisition burden from inference cost. The individual controls are established practice \cite{caicedo2017}; our aim is to quantify how far each can be trusted under treatment and experiment shift when applied to unseen stimuli.

\section{Materials and Methods}
\subsection{Data and functional labels}
We use released RxRx2 HUVEC metadata and image embeddings \cite{rxrx2}, and the raw six-channel images of all sites of the analysed treatments. Functional labels were curated by the authors and verified by a professor of pharmacy (Table~\ref{tab:mapping}); type-III interferons (IL-28A, IL-28B, IL-29) were excluded because of limited expected HUVEC responsiveness. Each treatment is applied at six concentrations in two experiments (HUVEC-1, HUVEC-2), giving 444 conditions with six replicate wells each and 10,642 image sites on 24 plates.

\subsection{Evaluation protocols and models}
All experiments hold out entire treatments. \emph{Embedding experiments} use five-fold stratified group cross-validation with treatment as the group. \emph{Raw-image experiments} use one fixed split of 23 training, 5 validation, and 9 test treatments, covering every group, because repeated CNN training was computationally impractical; absolute scores from the two protocols are not directly comparable. We report macro-F1, balanced accuracy (BA), and expected calibration error (ECE). Confidence intervals use a treatment-level cluster bootstrap (1,000 resamples); comparisons evaluate both settings on the same resampled treatments, giving paired intervals.

On embeddings we benchmark standardized, class-weighted Logistic Regression; XGBoost \cite{chen2016} with and without class weighting, to expose the effect of class imbalance (250 trees, depth 4, learning rate 0.06, row and column subsampling 0.85); and a small MLP (64 and 32 hidden units, class-balanced resampling, early stopping). Principal component analysis for dimensional reduction is fitted inside each training fold. Latency is measured per site on a laptop CPU (Intel Core Ultra 7 255H) together with the serialized model size. For raw images, we fine-tune ImageNet-pretrained MobileNetV3-Small \cite{howard2019} whose first convolution is replaced by a $C$-channel layer initialized from the channel-averaged RGB filter scaled by $3/C$, with a four-way classifier. Sites are resized to $224\times224$, augmented with random flips and $90^\circ$ rotations, and sampled with class-balanced weights. Training uses AdamW (learning rate and weight decay $10^{-4}$), batch size 32, and up to 25 epochs with early stopping (patience 5) on validation performance. Six channels are compared with DNA/RNA/mitochondria and DNA/mitochondria subsets, fixed before inspecting test results; six- and three-channel models use two seeds, and the two-channel model is exploratory with one.

\subsection{Reliability analyses}
\emph{Replicate aggregation} (Logistic): sites are averaged into wells and $k$ wells into a condition embedding; for $k\in\{1,2,3\}$ wells are drawn at random ten times, while $k=6$ uses all wells. Low and high strata are the two lowest and two highest of each treatment's six concentrations. \emph{Plate awareness} (Logistic): we compare same- versus cross-plate similarity of same-condition wells and evaluate unlabeled plate centering, which subtracts each plate's mean embedding. \emph{Deferral} (class-weighted XGBoost, the best-calibrated model with ECE 0.013 versus 0.030 unweighted): a site is accepted when its maximum class probability exceeds $\tau$ \cite{geifman2017}. In each outer fold, one training treatment per group is withheld as a calibration set, and the lowest $\tau$ with calibration-set error (site-level $1-$accuracy) at most 0.40 is applied unchanged to the unseen test fold. Deferral is scored throughout by the BA of accepted sites (equivalently, balanced error $1-\mathrm{BA}$), so that it cannot improve by favoring the majority group; the unweighted model is reported for comparison. \emph{Experiment shift}: within the same treatment-held-out folds, models are trained on one HUVEC experiment and tested on the other, in both directions. We compare raw embeddings with two label-free centerings applied to each experiment: subtracting the mean of all its analysed sites, and subtracting the mean of its untreated control (EMPTY) sites (about 3,790 per experiment), which does not depend on which treatments a batch contains. Aggregation and nested deferral are repeated in this setting, with calibration treatments and $\tau$ taken from the source experiment only. Because coverage then differs between representations, deferral is also compared at matched coverage and by the area under the balanced-error--coverage curve over 20--100\% coverage (AURC). \emph{Combined policy}: each condition is imaged one well at a time with a class-weighted Logistic model trained on random 1--6-well averages of training conditions. A fixed-$k$ policy averages $k$ wells and defers the least confident conditions to reach a target coverage; a sequential policy accepts at the first $k$ whose confidence reaches $\tau$ and defers after six wells. Cost is the mean number of wells imaged per condition, and results are averaged over ten random well orders. \emph{Diagnostics}: a three-class variant merging chemokine and angiogenic groups, and a binary chemokine-versus-angiogenic classifier.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Embedding baselines and group-specific behavior}
Logistic Regression reaches BA 0.442, within 0.004 of the best model (class-weighted XGBoost, 0.446), with a 6.1~KB model and 0.47~ms per site, while class weighting improves XGBoost's macro-F1 (0.458) and calibration (ECE 0.013) at unchanged cost (Table~\ref{tab:embedding}); this class-weighted model is used for deferral. The small MLP is poorly calibrated (ECE 0.477) and therefore unsuitable for confidence-based deferral. Treatment-level intervals are wide and overlap across models, so the ranking should not be over-interpreted. Reducing the Logistic input to 32 principal components retains BA (0.446), whereas 16 components lower it to 0.431; the post-embedding decision layer can therefore be compact, although this does not reduce the cost of producing the embedding.

A dedicated chemokine-versus-angiogenic classifier is near chance (BA 0.510, ROC-AUC 0.521), whereas merging the two groups gives a three-class Logistic BA of 0.578 (CI 0.431--0.705; chance 0.333). In HUVEC, chemokine and angiogenic stimuli thus do not produce distinguishable morphology in the released embeddings.

\subsection{Replicate aggregation under treatment shift}
Averaging all six wells instead of one raises condition-level BA from 0.416 to 0.465 (Fig.~\ref{fig:replicates}), a paired gain of $+0.049$ (CI 0.005--0.096). The gain is larger in the low-concentration stratum (0.294 to 0.396) than in the high stratum (0.499 to 0.509); the paired difference between the two gains is 0.12 (CI 0.02--0.23), so replicate acquisition is most useful where morphology signals are weak. The same concentration dependence appears at the single-site level, where unweighted XGBoost BA over absolute-concentration tertiles rises from 0.282 (low) to 0.477 (mid) and 0.550 (high).

\subsection{Plate awareness and confidence-based deferral}
Same-condition wells are more similar on the same plate (cosine 0.885) than across plates (0.862). Plate centering raises Logistic BA from 0.443 to 0.450 in a re-run of the centering pipeline, a small but consistent paired gain ($+0.007$, CI 0.002--0.012).

Pooling the five outer folds, nested deferral with class-weighted XGBoost accepts 53\% of sites and raises their BA from 0.443 (all sites, nested models) to 0.543 ($+0.100$, CI 0.048--0.120); within every fold, accepted BA exceeded that fold's own all-site BA (Fig.~\ref{fig:riskcoverage}, segments). Group coverage ranges from 46\% to 64\%, and angiogenic recall among accepted sites reaches 0.26. The unweighted model gains less ($+0.054$, CI 0.023--0.095, at 69\% coverage) and leaves angiogenic recall at 0.05, so class weighting matters for group-fair deferral. The selection rule remains sensitive to its small calibration sets of four treatments: thresholds ranged from 0.40 to 0.45 and coverage from 36\% to 64\% across folds, and accepted-site error exceeded the 0.40 calibration-set target in three folds. On pooled out-of-fold predictions, accepted BA rises steadily from 0.446 at full coverage to 0.542 at 57\% and 0.616 at 36\% coverage. We therefore treat deferral as exploratory re-measurement triage rather than a fixed decision rule.

\subsection{Combining aggregation and deferral}\label{sec:policy}
Table~\ref{tab:policy} combines the two controls under treatment shift. Averaging three wells and deferring the least confident quarter of conditions reaches an accepted BA of 0.499, not significantly different from imaging all six wells and accepting every condition (0.477; paired difference $+0.022$, CI $-0.017$ to 0.057) at half the wells; deferral itself adds $+0.042$ (CI 0.020--0.060) at three wells. Six wells with deferral at 67\% coverage reach 0.547, so the operator can trade imaging cost against coverage. In contrast, a sequential policy that adds wells one at a time and stops once confidence reaches $\tau=0.75$ uses 2.9 wells on average but reaches only 0.473 at 67\% coverage, 0.043 below three-well aggregation with deferral at the same coverage (CI $-0.068$ to $-0.017$). Confidence from one or two wells is too noisy to stop on: overconfident early errors are accepted before averaging can correct them, so wells should be aggregated before deferral is applied. The simulation classifier is trained on 1--6-well averages, so its six-well BA (0.477) differs slightly from Fig.~\ref{fig:replicates}.

\subsection{Reliability controls under experiment shift}
Transferring across experiments lowers treatment-held-out BA to 0.344--0.402 (Table~\ref{tab:shift}), compared with 0.438--0.442 when both experiments are pooled. Seeing a treatment in the other experiment does not compensate: a Logistic model trained on all HUVEC-1 treatments reaches 0.374 on HUVEC-2, versus 0.431 for unseen treatments under the pooled treatment-held-out model evaluated on the same HUVEC-2 sites (paired difference $-0.057$, CI $-0.106$ to 0.0004, upper bound $\approx0$), and 0.436 versus 0.454 on HUVEC-1 sites in the reverse direction ($-0.019$, CI $-0.065$ to 0.046).

The controls that helped under treatment shift lose most of their benefit. Averaging six wells changes condition-level BA from 0.340 to 0.323 ($-0.016$, CI $-0.096$ to 0.060) on raw embeddings and by $+0.011$ (CI $-0.028$ to 0.052) after control-well centering. Aggregation thus shows no detectable gain, although with half as many test conditions these intervals cannot exclude a gain of the size seen under treatment shift. Both label-free centerings recover site-level BA by 0.026--0.049 (Table~\ref{tab:shift}), with control-well centering on par with all-site centering. For deferral with class-weighted XGBoost, the source-calibrated threshold accepts 88\% of raw-embedding sites but 65\% after control-well centering, and accepted BA rises by 0.012 (CI 0.004--0.029) versus 0.052 (CI 0.025--0.093). Much of this difference reflects coverage: at a matched 65\% coverage, the gain is 0.039 on raw and 0.053 on control-centered embeddings (difference 0.014, CI $-0.001$ to 0.027). Confidence therefore still ranks sites under experiment shift, but centering reduces the area under the balanced-error--coverage curve (AURC 0.567 versus 0.508; reduction 0.059, CI 0.026--0.085) and lets the source threshold select a selective operating point without target labels. Removing the experiment offset is thus the prerequisite control under experiment shift, and additional wells did not substitute for it here.

\subsection{Reduced-channel raw-image screening}
The six-channel configuration gave the highest observed scores and was stable across seeds (Table~\ref{tab:channels}, Fig.~\ref{fig:channels}); reducing to DNA/RNA/mitochondria lowered BA from 0.398 to 0.362, and the single-seed two-channel result does not rank two versus three channels. Because only the first convolution depends on the channel count, all configurations have 1.52~M parameters, compute falls only from 60.9 to 55.5 and 53.7~MMACs, and single-thread CPU latency from 7.9 to 7.6 and 7.5~ms per site. The saving of reduced-channel imaging lies in stains, optics, acquisition time, and image data volume (one half or one third of the six-channel data), not in inference, and it costs measurable accuracy. On this split, complementary multi-organelle information therefore remains beneficial for screening unseen treatments.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
Chemokine and angiogenic stimuli are confused regardless of classifier, so a screening policy should report group-specific reliability, and a three-group formulation may suit this cell type better. The reliability gains under treatment shift are modest and their intervals wide because only 37 treatments are available; they should be confirmed on larger panels, and the deferral threshold requires larger calibration sets before it can be fixed in advance. Under experiment shift, label-free centering mainly restored the overall accuracy and operating point of deferral rather than its ranking ability, and aggregation showed no detectable gain, with intervals too wide to exclude one. In operation, each new batch should therefore include untreated control wells for centering; all-site centering performed similarly here, but it assumes a batch whose treatment composition resembles the training data, which a batch dominated by one group would violate. In our two experiments the source-calibrated threshold transferred after centering, but with so few experiments we cannot guarantee this in general; periodically including a few reference treatments with known labels would allow the threshold to be checked or re-calibrated. Only two HUVEC experiments are available, so experiment shift on independently acquired systems remains to be measured. The raw-image comparison uses a single split with nine test treatments; seed variation is small, but variation across alternative splits is not captured. All images come from a high-content research platform, so the channel ablation emulates, but does not demonstrate, a lower-cost imager. The system should be framed as candidate screening and re-measurement prioritization, not clinical diagnosis or definitive mechanism annotation.

\section{Conclusion}
For morphology-based screening of unseen stimuli, the central question is not only accuracy but when a prediction can be trusted. Under treatment shift, replicate aggregation and confidence-based deferral yield modest but statistically supported gains, plate centering adds a small consistent improvement, and aggregating three wells before deferring reaches accuracy comparable to six wells at half the imaging cost, whereas stopping early on single-well confidence does not. Under experiment shift, these controls lose most of their benefit; centering each experiment on its untreated control wells reduces the area under the balanced-error--coverage curve and restores a selective deferral operating point without target labels, whereas no aggregation gain is detectable. Group-specific reporting further reveals that chemokine and angiogenic responses are not separable in HUVEC morphology. Reducing six channels to three or two lowers stain, optics, acquisition, and storage burden at a measurable accuracy cost while leaving inference cost unchanged. Future work should validate the policy prospectively on larger panels and lower-cost imaging systems.
