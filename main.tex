\begin{abstract}
High-content cell imaging can support early-stage perturbation screening, but morphology-based predictions may be unstable across experimental plates, concentrations, replicates, and previously unseen treatments. We study reliability-aware broad functional screening of 37 immune and vascular stimuli in public RxRx2 HUVEC Cell Painting data. The primary protocol holds out treatments, preventing images from the same stimulus from appearing in both training and testing. Released 128-dimensional embeddings support moderate four-class screening performance, while a compact Logistic model preserves performance after dimensional reduction. More importantly, aggregation across replicate wells improves condition-level balanced accuracy from 0.416 to 0.465, with the largest gain under low within-treatment concentration. We further quantify plate-associated similarity differences and evaluate an unlabeled plate-centering baseline. A confidence-aware defer policy reduces error among accepted predictions while referring uncertain cases for re-measurement. In raw-image experiments, ImageNet-pretrained MobileNetV3-Small achieved $0.398\pm0.002$ balanced accuracy with all six fluorescence channels across two seeds; reducing the input to DNA, RNA, and mitochondrial channels reduced balanced accuracy to $0.362\pm0.003$. These results position reliability controls and explicit acquisition--accuracy trade-offs as practical components of lightweight phenotype-screening pipelines.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
High-content microscopy converts cellular responses to perturbations into images and learned representations. Cell Painting provides multiplexed structural measurements that support mechanism-oriented screening, but its biological signal can be confounded by experimental and imaging variation \cite{cimini2023,arevalo2024}. A practical screening pipeline must decide not only which broad response group is predicted, but also whether the prediction is reliable enough to act on. This work evaluates treatment-held-out functional screening, reliability controls, and reduced-channel acquisition for public HUVEC Cell Painting data.

\section{Materials and Methods}
\subsection{Data and functional labels}
We use released RxRx2 HUVEC metadata, image embeddings, and a selected raw six-channel image subset \cite{rxrx2}. Pharmacy-reviewed labels group 37 treatments into type I/II interferons, IL-1/TNF-superfamily cytokines, CXC/CC chemokines, and angiogenic/vascular-regulatory factors. Type-III interferon ligands IL-28A, IL-28B, and IL-29 are excluded because of limited expected HUVEC responsiveness.

\subsection{Evaluation and models}
Treatments are held out between training, validation, and test sets. We benchmark Logistic Regression, XGBoost, and a small MLP on released embeddings. For raw images, we fine-tune an ImageNet-pretrained MobileNetV3-Small with an expanded fluorescence input stem. We compare all six channels against a three-channel DNA/RNA/mitochondria configuration and a two-channel DNA/mitochondria configuration. Six- and three-channel models are repeated with two independent random seeds; the two-channel configuration is exploratory with one seed. Reliability analyses include well aggregation, plate-associated similarity, unlabeled plate centering, and confidence-based deferral.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Embedding baselines and compact deployment}
The released embeddings yield moderate treatment-held-out performance. XGBoost attains Macro-F1 0.447, whereas Logistic Regression attains the highest balanced accuracy (0.442) with an approximately 8~KB serialized model. Reducing the Logistic input from 128 to 32 dimensions retains balanced accuracy near 0.440.

\subsection{Replicate aggregation is most valuable under low concentration}
Condition-level aggregation improves balanced accuracy from 0.416 with one well to 0.465 with six wells. The improvement is larger in the low within-treatment concentration subset (0.294 to 0.396), indicating that replicate acquisition is especially useful when morphology signals are weak.

\subsection{Plate shift and confidence-aware triage}
Within-condition embedding similarity is higher for same-plate pairs (0.885) than cross-plate pairs (0.862). Unlabeled plate centering offers a modest Logistic balanced-accuracy increase from 0.443 to 0.450. Confidence-based deferral reduces accepted-case error but exhibits fold variability, so it is interpreted as an exploratory re-measurement triage policy.

\subsection{Reduced-channel raw-image screening}
Table~\ref{tab:channels} and Fig.~\ref{fig:channels} summarize raw-image ablations under the same treatment-held-out protocol. The six-channel configuration was strongest and stable across seeds (balanced accuracy $0.398\pm0.002$). Reducing input to DNA/RNA/mitochondria lowered balanced accuracy to $0.362\pm0.003$ and Macro-F1 to $0.348\pm0.020$. The two-channel DNA/mitochondria setting obtained similar single-seed balanced accuracy (0.365), but this result is exploratory and should not be used to rank two versus three channels. Thus, reduced acquisition lowered computational burden, but complementary multi-organelle information remained beneficial for robust screening of unseen treatments.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
The four broad groups are biologically reviewed but are not equally separable in released HUVEC morphology embeddings: the chemokine--angiogenic binary diagnostic is near chance. Results also decrease under HUVEC domain shift. The reduced-channel experiment uses a subset of a high-content research assay and does not demonstrate operation on a consumer-grade microscope. Therefore, the system should be framed as reliability-aware candidate screening and re-measurement prioritization, not clinical diagnosis, definitive mechanism annotation, or a deployed consumer device.

\section{Conclusion}
Replicate aggregation, batch-awareness, compact classifiers, and uncertainty-aware referral provide useful controls for lightweight morphology-based screening under treatment shift.
